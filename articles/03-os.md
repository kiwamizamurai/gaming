# OS options

Operating systems for handheld game consoles divide broadly into Android-based and Linux-based. Which you choose changes the software you can play, how fast the device boots, and how easy it is to modify. This page covers the differences between them and sorts out the OSes that can actually be swapped in. The installation steps themselves are separated out into [Installing CFW](04-install-cfw.md).

## Differences between Android and Linux

A CSDN article by someone who built their own retro device compares the two in a table. According to this article, Android devices take 20–40 seconds to boot, and the look of the screen is not unified across apps. On the other hand, PS2 emulators are available, as are unofficial Switch builds, cloud gaming, and Steam Link. Linux-based systems boot within 5 seconds, have a unified, console-like look, and let you change settings freely. However, you have to configure BIOS files and cores yourself ([天马G前端的使用](https://blog.csdn.net/fanged/article/details/152960565)).

The remaining rows of the table show further differences. Systems such as PSP, NDS, and GBA can be played on either OS. On Android, each emulator's APK is called, so the screens are fragmented. On Linux, cores are built into the OS and the screens are unified. For cloud gaming, Android is "fully supported" and Linux is "Moonlight only (if the core is there)". For system freedom, the assessment is that Android is hard to customize because of root permission restrictions, while Linux can be modified as much as you like ([天马G前端的使用](https://blog.csdn.net/fanged/article/details/152960565)).

After the table, the writer says Android looks slightly better. The reason is that, in addition to the emulators anyone can play, it can also play games made for Android. The writer also distinguishes the audiences: Android suits "people who want to use emulators, cloud gaming, and apps", and Linux suits "people who want the feeling of a game console the moment they turn on the power" ([天马G前端的使用](https://blog.csdn.net/fanged/article/details/152960565)). On the difference in look, Android is described as "like an Android TV box" and Linux as "like the firmware of a genuine game console".

The same article also explains why PS2 emulators lean toward Android. PS2 emulators implement JIT using the Android NDK and Vulkan or OpenGL ES, and that part is hard to port to Linux. The article goes on to ask why some emulators run only on Android when Android is itself a modified Linux, and reports, after looking into it, that this comes down to the ecosystem and personal circumstances. Specifically, the author of the PS2 emulator developed only the Android version, AetherSX2 ([天马G前端的使用](https://blog.csdn.net/fanged/article/details/152960565)).

JIT is a technique that converts the instructions of the game being played into the instructions of the machine running it during execution, and it determines the speed of heavy emulation such as PS2 and Switch. In the article's explanation, this also includes finding frequently executed parts and generating faster machine code. The article also calls JIT just-in-time compilation (即时编译 in Chinese) or a dynamic recompiler (Dynarec), and says that the hardest part of a retro device is the emulator ([天马G前端的使用](https://blog.csdn.net/fanged/article/details/152960565)).

## Frontends used on Android

On Android handhelds, emulators are separate apps. The screen widely used in China for launching them all from one place is a frontend called 天马G (Pegasus). The CSDN writer says that within China, almost all players and the workshops that sell devices use this frontend. Installation is divided into three stages: the app, the settings, and the ROMs ([天马G前端的使用](https://blog.csdn.net/fanged/article/details/152960565)).

天马G is not itself an emulator. The same article explains that 天马G only acts as ROM management software and an emulator launcher, leaving the actual processing to each emulator's APK. Because it is built with Qt, it also supports many OSes. On Android, the article says, citing the source code, that it works by assembling arguments and calling another app with startActivity ([天马G前端的使用](https://blog.csdn.net/fanged/article/details/152960565)).

The official Pegasus documentation gives an explanation along the same lines in its Android section. It says that Pegasus runs on Android 5.0 and later, is distributed as an APK, and uses `<storage>/pegasus-frontend` as its settings folder. For launching other apps, it gives an example using the Activity Manager's `am start` ([Android documentation](https://github.com/mmatyas/pegasus-docs/blob/master/docs/user-guide/platform-android.md)).

Android 11 and later have a restriction that prevents ordinary file manager apps from writing to each app's data folder. The CSDN article says that because of this restriction, some 天马G settings have to be placed by hand. The Pegasus documentation also says that on Android 11 and later, per-directory permissions may be required ([format documentation](https://github.com/mmatyas/pegasus-docs/blob/master/docs/user-guide/meta-files.md)). Details of how to use the frontend are covered in the [Pegasus article](05-frontend-tianma-g.md).

## ROCKNIX

ROCKNIX is an immutable Linux distribution for handheld game consoles. The [official Wiki](https://rocknix.org/) lists these features: local and remote multiplayer, touch controls, power and performance profiles, Bluetooth, HDMI output, cloud and LAN sync through Syncthing and rclone, VPN through WireGuard, Tailscale, and ZeroTier, RetroAchievements, and automatic retrieval of game information. According to the [repository README](https://github.com/ROCKNIX/distribution), ROCKNIX is a fork of JELOS. On licensing, the official Wiki says it includes components limited to non-commercial use.

![The ROCKNIX system selection screen. Source: the ROCKNIX repository](https://rocknix.org/_inc/images/screenshots/system-view.png)

The [supported device list](https://rocknix.org/devices/) includes many Anbernic devices (from the RG351 series to the RG40XX H/V and RG CubeXX), AYANEO's Pocket series, AYN's Odin 2, Odin 2 Mini, Odin 2 Portal, Thor, and Odin 3, many Powkiddy devices, and Retroid's Pocket 5, Pocket 6, Flip 2, Mini, and Nova, among others. The RG406V, RG556, and Retroid Pocket 4 Pro are not on the list. The Retroid Pocket 5 page has steps for rewriting Android's bootloader (ABL) during installation. The installation steps are covered in [Installing CFW](04-install-cfw.md).

The overview table on the Retroid Pocket 5 page shows what is inside the OS. The SoC is a Snapdragon 865 (SM8250), and the kernel is mainline Linux. The GPU driver is Freedreno, the Vulkan driver is turnip, and the display environment is Sway and EmulationStation ([Retroid Pocket 5](https://rocknix.org/devices/retroid/retroid-pocket-5/)).

The same page says that fan settings can be changed globally, per device, and per game, and also covers joystick LED color, the battery level display, turning vibration on and off, and Bluetooth audio and controller support. The dual-screen add-on is currently supported only through manual configuration. By default, ROCKNIX detects the controller and sets RetroArch hotkeys automatically. That configuration is overwritten each time RetroArch is launched, so to keep your own settings, turn off the "AUTOCONFIGURE RETROARCH HOTKEYS" option ([Retroid Pocket 5](https://rocknix.org/devices/retroid/retroid-pocket-5/)).

For devices without hardware suspend, ROCKNIX has a feature called "fake suspend". When you press the power button, or close the lid on a clamshell device, the LEDs, audio, and controls stop and the screen turns off. The game and EmulationStation processes are frozen, and the CPU and GPU are set to power saving. CPU cores other than core 0 are stopped. The advantage is that the game you were playing stays in a low-power state and can be resumed right away ([Fake suspend](https://rocknix.org/configure/fake-suspend/)).

The time until the device automatically powers off is 0–30 minutes outside a game (default 0 minutes) and 0–30 minutes during a game (default 15 minutes). At 0 minutes, the power goes off immediately. When a charger is connected, the suspend behavior still happens but the power does not go off. When an HDMI cable is connected, neither the suspend behavior nor the power-off happens, so a device with its lid closed can be used like a dock ([Fake suspend](https://rocknix.org/configure/fake-suspend/)).

There are three ways to update: OTA, a manual `.tar` file, and development builds. For OTA, in "System Settings" in the EmulationStation main menu, choose "Start Update" under the "System Update" heading. To update manually, get the `.tar` for your device, copy it to the device's `/storage/.update` or the Samba "update" share, and restart, and the update begins. Development builds do not include credentials for scraping and RetroAchievements, so those features do not work ([Update](https://rocknix.org/play/update/)).

## Knulli

Knulli is custom firmware forked from Batocera. According to the [official documentation](https://github.com/knulli-cfw/knulli.org/blob/main/docs/about-knulli.md), it adds to Batocera support for handhelds that do not work with the standard Linux kernel. Its approach to settings is distinctive: emulators are configured per system or per game in EmulationStation, not in the RetroArch menu. At every boot, Knulli generates per-game emulator settings from the EmulationStation settings.

The folder structure is strictly defined and is case-sensitive. When moving an existing game collection to Knulli, it has to be made to fit this structure. According to the [explanation of game storage](https://github.com/knulli-cfw/knulli.org/blob/main/docs/play/add-games/game-storage.md), all data goes under `/userdata`. Under it are folders such as `bios`, `roms`, `saves`, `themes`, and `system`. Inside `roms` are subfolders for each system, and games placed outside `roms` are not recognized.

![Knulli booting on an RG40XX H. Source: the Knulli documentation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/knulli-cfw/knulli.org/main/docs/_inc/images/knulli-booting-rg40xx-h.png)

Knulli comes with many features out of the box. [About Knulli](https://github.com/knulli-cfw/knulli.org/blob/main/docs/about-knulli.md) lists emulators and ports (including PortMaster), support for the native version of PICO-8, information retrieval from ScreenScraper, TheGamesDB, and ArcadeDB, RetroAchievements, Bluetooth, HDMI output, wireless file adding over SMB, screen borders (bezels), and themes. Dynamic and handmade collections, Syncthing, RGB LEDs, SSH, and behavior on closing the lid of a clamshell device are also listed.

The RG40XX H page describes what is inside the device. The SoC is an Allwinner H700, the kernel is Allwinner BSP 4.9.170, the GPU is a Mali G31, and the interface is EmulationStation. It has Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, suspend (a short press of the power button), and HDMI, and supported USB Wi-Fi dongles can also be used. According to the page, Anbernic has not published the u-boot and kernel source code for this device. The kernel, bootloader, and u-boot in the release are therefore taken from the stock firmware ([RG40XX H](https://github.com/knulli-cfw/knulli.org/blob/main/docs/devices/anbernic/rg40xx-h.md)).

The [supported device list](https://github.com/knulli-cfw/knulli.org/blob/main/docs/devices/index.md) contains Anbernic's H700 devices (RG28XX, RG34XX, RG35XX series, RG40XX H/V, RG CubeXX), Retroid's Pocket 5 and Pocket Mini, Miyoo's Flip, Powkiddy's RGB30 and X55, and TrimUI's Brick and Smart Pro. However, some devices on the list have no published build. The Knulli documentation warns against using another device's image on a device with no build of its own.

The main project is [knulli-cfw/knulli-linux](https://github.com/knulli-cfw/knulli-linux). The old repository, [knulli-cfw/distribution](https://github.com/knulli-cfw/distribution), was used up to the "Gladiator II" release, and development has moved to the new repository.

## muOS

muOS is developed under [MustardOS](https://github.com/MustardOS) on GitHub. The frontend, internal components, themes, and tools are in separate repositories. An article on 什么值得买 labeled AIGC explains that on H700 Anbernic devices, muOS is for those who want simplicity and Knulli for those who want features. It also says that muOS prioritizes RetroArch and boots quickly ([2026开源掌机红黑榜](https://post.smzdm.com/p/aww5ev54/)). The official site, muos.dev, could not be read under the permission settings of the browser used in this research, so the official description could not be confirmed.

## ArkOS and JELOS

[ArkOS](https://github.com/christianhaitian/arkos) is an OS for Rockchip devices. Its README says only "Another rockchip Operating System" and directs readers to the [Wiki](https://github.com/christianhaitian/arkos/wiki) for details. [JELOS](https://github.com/JustEnoughLinuxOS/distribution) is described as "Home of the JELOS Linux distribution", and its last update was in May 2024. ROCKNIX is a fork of JELOS, and the ROCKNIX repository was still being updated in October 2026.

## GammaOS Core and LineageOS

On the Android side too, some devices can have their OS swapped. [GammaOS Core](https://github.com/TheGammaSqueeze/GammaOSCore) is a minimal Android 13 TV build based on LineageOS, intended for low-performance chips such as the Rockchip RK3566. Supported devices listed include Anbernic's RG353 series and RG503, Powkiddy's RGB30 and X55, GKD's Bubble, Miyoo's Flip, and TrimUI's Smart Pro. It can boot from an SD card, so the system on the internal storage can be left as it is. It includes RetroArch and the launcher Daijisho, and also supports root and Magisk.

The Retroid Pocket Nova is among the devices officially supported by [LineageOS](https://wiki.lineageos.org/devices/RPN). The page shows the SoC as a Qualcomm Dragonwing QCS8550 and the release date as July 2026. 掌机圈 lists the same device as a Snapdragon 8 Gen 2.

## How to think about choosing

The options for each device can be summarized as follows. On the RG40XXH and RG35XX series, you can choose ROCKNIX, Knulli, or muOS. On the Retroid Pocket 5, you can install ROCKNIX and Knulli in addition to the stock Android. The RG406V and RG556 are not on the ROCKNIX supported list, so the basic approach is to use the stock Android.

The criterion for choosing an OS is the systems whose games you want to play. For heavy emulation such as PS2 and Switch, Android emulators are ahead. For easy play of FC, SFC, GBA, PS1, and so on, Linux-based OSes are light and fast and have a unified look.

Whichever OS you install, rewriting the bootloader or internal storage always carries a risk of breaking the device. The ROCKNIX steps require you to back up the current ABL before rewriting it.
